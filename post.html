EVENT CONTRACT BASICS

A market price is a live view — not a guarantee.

Prices move as traders react to new information, liquidity and changing expectations.

ILLUSTRATIVE MARKET Will Event A happen before the deadline?
YES · 63¢NO · 37¢

Prediction markets turn questions about future events into contracts that can be priced and traded. The easiest way to understand them is to focus on three things: what the contract says, what the current price represents and how the market resolves.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • A Yes/No contract usually settles according to a predefined outcome and resolution source.
  • A price such as 63¢ is often read as roughly 63% implied probability, but it is still a market price rather than a promise.
  • Fees, spread, liquidity and settlement rules can change the experience even when two platforms list similar questions.

What is a prediction market?

A prediction market is a market built around the outcome of a future event. Instead of buying a share in a company, you take a position on a specific question. That question might be about politics, sports, economics, technology, weather or another measurable event.

The contract rules matter more than the headline. A well-designed market states exactly what counts as a Yes result, what counts as a No result, the deadline and the source used to resolve the outcome.

How Yes and No contracts work

Many event contracts use a binary structure. You can take the Yes side if you think the stated event will happen, or the No side if you think it will not.

Simple example

Imagine a Yes contract is trading at 63¢. If the event later resolves Yes under the contract rules, a winning contract may settle at $1. If it resolves No, the Yes contract may settle at $0. The exact mechanics depend on the platform and contract terms.

What does a 63¢ price mean?

People often read 63¢ as approximately a 63% market-implied probability. That is a useful mental model, but the price is still created by market activity. It can move because participants disagree, new information arrives or liquidity changes.

A market can also have a wide spread between the price available to buy and the price available to sell. That is why the displayed headline probability should not be the only number you check.

Why prices move

Prediction-market prices move for many of the same reasons other market prices move: new evidence, changing expectations and differences between buyers and sellers. A fresh poll, injury report, economic release or official announcement can move a contract quickly.

  • New information: data changes how participants view the event.
  • Liquidity: thin markets can move more sharply from a small number of orders.
  • Time: uncertainty can rise or fall as the resolution date gets closer.
  • Rules: clarification about the contract wording can affect pricing.

How settlement works

When a market reaches its resolution point, the platform applies the contract rules and named resolution source. This is where careful reading becomes important. Two questions that sound nearly identical can settle differently if their rules use different dates, thresholds or official sources.

Important: read the full contract terms before relying on the market title alone. The title is a summary; the resolution criteria control the outcome.

Fees, spread and liquidity

Your result is not determined only by whether your view was correct. Trading fees, exchange fees, spread and the price at which you can exit a position can all matter. Some platforms use a simple fee structure while others calculate fees according to price, contract type or exchange venue.

Liquidity matters because it tells you whether enough participants are available around the price you want. Headline platform volume does not necessarily mean every individual market is deep.

What to check before placing a trade

  • Exact contract question and resolution criteria.
  • Named source used to determine the final outcome.
  • Current Yes/No price and bid/ask spread.
  • Available size or market depth.
  • Trading and exchange fees.
  • Your location eligibility and account requirements.
  • Whether the position can be closed before settlement.

Bottom line

Prediction markets are easiest to understand when you treat the contract wording as the foundation. The price can help summarize the market's current view, but it changes continuously and does not remove uncertainty. Before using any platform, compare its rules, fees, liquidity, access requirements and settlement process rather than choosing on the headline probability alone.
